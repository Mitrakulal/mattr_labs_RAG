# ===== FAQ: About the Company =====
## route: /about, /

Q: What is mattrlabs?
A: mattrlabs is a full-stack engineering studio that designs and ships real
products — mechanical, electronics, firmware, and AI — all under one roof.

Q: What does mattrlabs actually do?
A: mattrlabs works three ways: they build their own intelligent hardware
products (flagship: an AI-powered PTZ camera), they take on contract
engineering work across software/computer vision, embedded firmware,
electronics, and mechanical/CAD, and they offer a 3D-printing service where
you upload a model and they print and ship it.

Q: Is mattrlabs a big company?
A: mattrlabs describes itself as "a small team" and "a compact engineering
studio." The site highlights four core disciplines, each led by a founding
engineer, rather than describing a large organization.

Q: What makes mattrlabs different from other engineering firms?
A: Most teams stitch together a separate mechanical firm, electronics firm,
and software firm. mattrlabs owns the whole chain — CAD, electronics,
embedded, and AI — in one team, in the order a smart device is actually built.

Q: Does mattrlabs build its own products, or only work for clients?
A: Both. Their flagship own product is the AI PTZ camera, which they build
"in public." They also take on contract engineering work for clients, and
client case studies get added to their Work page as projects wrap.

Q: What are the four disciplines mattrlabs covers?
A: Software & Computer Vision, Embedded Systems & Firmware, Electronics &
Hardware, and Mechanical/CAD & 3D Printing. Each has its own founding engineer.

Q: Why should I trust mattrlabs to build my product?
A: Their own stated reasoning: they build their own hardware product (the PTZ
camera) rather than only advising — "we eat our own cooking" — and they
emphasize showing working demos rather than just claiming capability.

# ===== FAQ: The AI PTZ Camera =====
## route: /products/ptz-camera

Q: What is the AI PTZ camera?
A: An AI-powered pan-tilt-zoom camera that automatically tracks and frames a
subject, so one operator (or no operator at all) can run a professional-
looking video feed.

Q: Is the PTZ camera available to buy?
A: No — it's currently in development (alpha stage, working toward v1).
mattrlabs is lining up pilot users rather than selling it commercially yet.

Q: How do I get one of the early PTZ cameras?
A: Apply through the pilot program by using the "Join the pilot" link, which
goes to the Contact page — tell them your use-case to apply.

Q: What can the PTZ camera actually do?
A: Five main things: auto-tracking (locks onto and follows a person), auto-
framing (keeps the subject centred and composed while zooming), motorized
pan/tilt/zoom movement, on-device edge AI (no cloud round-trip needed), and
customizable/white-labelable tracking behavior.

Q: Does the PTZ camera need an internet connection to work?
A: No — its computer vision runs on-device (edge AI), meaning low latency and
no cloud round-trip; it works on your local network.

Q: What video resolution does the PTZ camera support?
A: The target spec is 1080p / 4K, though this is still listed as part of the
in-development target specs, not a finalized/shipping spec.

Q: What are the target specs for the PTZ camera?
A: Movement: motorized pan/tilt/zoom (exact range still TBD). Tracking:
on-device real-time computer vision. Compute: an edge AI module (on-device
inference). Video: 1080p/4K target. Interfaces: IP/USB/HDMI (TBD).
Customization: tunable tracking, with OEM/white-label options. Note: these are
explicitly described as still firming up as the product moves from alpha to v1.

Q: What is the PTZ camera used for?
A: Three main use cases: live streaming for small venues (churches, lecture
halls, local sports), auto-framing in hybrid meeting rooms so remote
participants always see the right speaker, and specialized monitoring (labs,
livestock, perimeters) with tracking plus alerts.

Q: Has the PTZ camera actually been tested anywhere real?
A: Yes — an early unit has already run a pilot in an actual lecture hall with
students present, mounted on a tripod. This is a real deployment that has
already happened, not just a planned future one.

Q: Does the PTZ camera work with video call software like Zoom or Google Meet?
A: The publicly shown demo specifically shows it running live during a Google
Meet call, with features like hand-raise detection (flagging who wants to
speak) and on-device face-recognition attendance tracking. Compatibility with
other platforms isn't specified on the site.

Q: Can I customize or white-label the PTZ camera?
A: Yes — the product page explicitly states the tracking behaviour can be
tuned for your use-case, or the whole product can be white-labelled.

Q: Is there a demo video of the PTZ camera?
A: Yes — the product page includes real prototype footage showing the camera's
tracking motion, plus real photos of the physical, in-house printed and
assembled prototype unit.

Q: Who builds the PTZ camera's tracking and vision features?
A: The Software & Computer Vision discipline, led by mattrlabs' founding
engineer in that area, builds the tracking, detection, and auto-framing
intelligence specifically.

# ===== FAQ: 3D Printing Service =====
## route: /3d-printing

Q: How does the 3D printing service work?
A: Three steps: upload your model (STL, OBJ, or 3MF), configure it (material,
colour, quality, infill, quantity), then get an instant price estimate before
requesting the actual print.

Q: What file formats does mattrlabs accept for 3D printing?
A: .stl, .obj, and .3mf files.

Q: Is there a file size limit for 3D printing uploads?
A: Yes — up to 50 MB.

Q: Do I get charged immediately when I upload a model?
A: No — your model is only previewed in your browser; nothing is uploaded
until you actually submit. The price shown at that stage is a rough estimate.

Q: Is the instant price quote final?
A: No — the instant estimate shown in the browser is rough. mattrlabs confirms
the final price and collects your actual file by email afterward.

Q: How do I actually submit my file for printing?
A: After previewing your model and getting the rough estimate in-browser, the
final quote and file collection happens by email — you don't upload the real
file until that step.

Q: What can I configure when ordering a 3D print?
A: Material, colour, quality, infill, and quantity.
